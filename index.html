Overview

Galah is a hybrid-propellant rocket designed to reach 30,000 ft. As a Flight Systems Engineer I work on the structures that hold the vehicle together: the truss between the oxidiser tank and combustion chamber, the bulkheads, and the composite airframe.

I've followed my parts through the full cycle: designing them in Onshape, checking them with FEA, building the composite sections by hand, and breaking test articles to confirm the simulations were right.

Aft truss and chamber adapter plate

Onshape, ANSYS, 6061-T6 aluminium

The aft truss is a three-strut 6061-T6 aluminium structure that sits between the oxidiser tank and the combustion chamber. It does two jobs at once: it is the structural load path through that part of the rocket, and it acts as the body coupler joining the airframe sections.

I designed the truss in Onshape and used FEA to drive the material choice. I also helped design and iterate the chamber adapter plate that the truss connects to.

Also designed

  • Forward truss
  • Nose cone, avionics and aft recovery bulkheads

Airframe, coupler and nose cone layups

8HS fibreglass, DowAksa A-38 carbon twill, Ampreg 31 epoxy

I was a core member of the layup team from start to finish, building the body tubes, couplers, boat tail and nose cone for Galah.

Process

  1. Prepared moulds with PVA release.
  2. Cut plies to pattern.
  3. Hand wet layup with Ampreg 31 epoxy, targeting a researched 60/40 fibre-to-resin ratio.
  4. Vacuum bagged each part and monitored the cure cycle.
  5. Inspected and trimmed each part after cure.

FEA and ANSYS simulation

ANSYS, mesh convergence studies

I ran the stress analysis on the truss, adapter plate and bulkheads in ANSYS, and used the results to choose materials.

To make sure the answers didn't depend on the mesh, I ran mesh convergence studies on each model. I then wrote up the simulation setup and results as documentation the rest of the team could review and reuse.

Destructive testing

Static and dynamic test machines, aluminium truss struts

To check the FEA against reality, I tested the aluminium truss struts to failure on static and dynamic machines, and wrote the test procedures myself.

I identified three failure modes across the tests: yield, buckling and thread shear-out. I then compared the measured failure loads with my FEA predictions to validate the design.
